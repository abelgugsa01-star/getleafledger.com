Reconcile tobacco import records with accounting before reporting

A supplier invoice, customs entry and accounting posting may describe the same shipment differently. LeafLedger helps organize supported records and identify gaps before they become unexplained figures in a report draft.

Collect the evidence behind the totals

  • Customs entry references and applicable CBP entry-summary records.
  • Supplier and accounting records with dates, product identifiers and units.
  • The applicable TTB reporting records and prior reviewed period.
  • Documented unit conversions and the evidence for excise tax amounts.

Example: units match, dates still differ

An invoice records 10 cases of 1,000 sticks each, while the customs record shows 10,000 sticks. The quantities reconcile after confirming the pack size. If accounting posts the invoice in the following month, keep that timing difference visible and apply the relevant reporting instructions; do not move every record into the invoice month merely to match totals.

Separate tax from other charges

A combined customs payment may include several types of charges. Trace the tobacco excise-tax component to supporting records instead of using the entire payment as tax. FDA's reporting guidance identifies applicable TTB forms and CBP 7501 as supporting documents. Read FDA's supporting-record guidance.

Review exceptions, then regenerate

Missing documents, inconsistent units, unidentified classes and period differences need review. Correct supported inputs and regenerate the draft; retain the earlier version and explanation. The output is a review package, not proof of filing or a regulatory approval.

Should CBP, accounting and TTB totals always be identical?

Only compare figures with the same scope, units and reporting basis. First explain timing, classification and charge differences; matching grand totals alone is not enough.
